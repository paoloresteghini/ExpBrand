Our foundation

A quality filter, not a marketplace.

Experiential Star is a hand-curated directory of the most unusual and awe-inspiring experiences in the world. A small editorial team chooses every one. Visitors find the one that grabs them and book it direct with the people who run it, so there is no middle-man fee.

We find the door, open it, then step aside.

  1. 01

    Curation is the product.

    Fewer, better, each one chosen by a person. A listing earns its place; volume never does.

  2. 02

    Discovery before destination.

    Most people arrive without a place in mind. Help them find what they did not know to look for.

  3. 03

    Send people onward.

    The win is the click to the provider. Never trap the visitor; there is always a way to continue.

  4. 04

    Honest about what we are.

    An editorial filter, not a travel agent. We do not book, hold availability or invent proof.

The idea at the centre

The Title-Card Wall.

A streaming catalogue at night: a deep ground, rails of upright posters and one thing in focus at a time. Whatever is in focus grows, says what it is, lends its colour to the room and is named in a bar at the foot of the screen. Everything else dims and waits.

Wordmark

The name, set wide.

The wordmark is pure type: Archivo extra-bold at its widest setting, in capitals. It is the only expanded setting in the whole system, which is what makes it recognisable against the condensed titles around it.

Experiential Star

Use the full name wherever Experiential Star is introduced. In running text, write it as Experiential Star, never ES or Exp Star.

Colour

Three steps of near-black ground, three steps of warm off-white ink, one amber, and whatever colour the posters bring. The interface owns no other hue. Click a swatch to copy its value.

Titles are condensed capitals.

Voice

Specific, onward, honest, quietly amazed.

The trips are extraordinary, so the words do not need to be. Say exactly what happens, where, and what it costs, then point the way to the people who run it.

  1. 01

    Specific.

    Name the place, the thing you do and the from-price. A real detail beats any adjective.

  2. 02

    Onward.

    Every piece of copy ends somewhere: the provider, the list, the next trip. We are the shortlist, not the destination.

  3. 03

    Honest.

    Do not present stale dates as current, unverified numbers as fact, or member perks we cannot show. Say what we are.

  4. 04

    Quietly amazed.

    The trip is the superlative. No shouting, no countdowns, no fake scarcity, very few exclamation marks.

Say this

Sleep in a glass igloo in Finnish Lapland and watch the northern lights from bed. From $3,500, booked direct.

Specific, sensory and onward. The reader knows where, what and how much.

Avoid this

The ULTIMATE bucket-list trip you NEED to book NOW! Only a few spots left!

Shouting, invented urgency and a promise of availability we do not hold.

Words we reach for

Hand-picked, book direct, the people who run it, from (price), worth the story, your list, find yours, full story.

Words we use with care

Luxury, exclusive, unique, once-in-a-lifetime, best, perks, 20,000 members (unverified), guaranteed, available.

Photography

The poster is the colour.

Photography is the only colour on the page. Show the place and the thing you do there, at the scale that makes it worth the trip. At rest it is held back; in focus it comes up to full colour. Hover a poster.

Show the experience.

The place, the activity, the moment. Wide enough to feel the scale, close enough to feel the detail.

Held back at rest.

Saturation 68%, brightness 80% until a poster takes focus. Then full colour and a slow 4% zoom.

Scrim, never a panel.

Text over photography sits on a bottom-up black scrim. No boxes, no blur cards.

Credit every borrowed image.

Licensed photography carries its author and licence in the credits panel. Never imply a stock image is a guest's own.

About the photographs in this guide: the original listing photography is unavailable, so the site uses openly licensed Wikimedia Commons images as placeholders, credited at the foot of this page and in the site footer. Replace them with provider-supplied photography as it is restored.

Title cards

No empty frames.

131 listings have no photograph. Each gets a title card: a gradient of its own dark colour with its name fitted to the frame. A title card is artwork, used everywhere a photograph would be. Never an icon, a pattern or a grey box.

The One Slate Rule.

A 165 degree gradient of the listing's colour, from slightly lifted to mixed toward black, title in ink.

Fit the card.

Motion

Cut in like a title sequence.

One grammar: width and position change on an exponential ease-out, colour drifts slowly, and each page opens with a short sequence in which the title is cut in from a clip rather than faded. Reduced motion collapses all of it.

What changed. What it means. One way forward.

  1. 01

    Start with what changed.

    "Your list is ready." The first line answers why this email exists.

  2. 02

    Show the trip.

    A poster or title card, the name in condensed capitals, country and from-price beneath. Real details, no adjectives doing the work.

  3. 03

    Name the action.

    "Open my list", "Sign in". Match the button to where it goes, and use one primary per email.

  4. 04

    Close briefly.

    A short sign-off from the team, then the footer. No second pitch below a service email.

Recognisable, even when simple.

Email clients strip a lot. These are the parts that must survive.

Reading column

600px wide, 24px side padding, one column. Stacks cleanly at phone width with no media queries needed.

Dark by default

Ground #0F1013 set as bgcolor on every table, so dark-mode clients have nothing to invert. Ink #F3F0E9 for titles, ink 2 for body.

Type that degrades well

Archivo where the client loads web fonts, then Arial Narrow, then Arial. Titles stay extra-bold capitals whatever the font.

One ink button

Emails keep people on site, so the primary is ink with near-black text. Amber appears only if a link goes straight to a provider, and only once.

Pictures with words

Hosted images with real alt text, 4px corners. Missing photo? Use the title card colour as a solid cell with the name in ink.

Close the loop

The footer says why the email arrived, links to preferences, and editorial sends carry an unsubscribe link.

Guardrails

Keep it focused. Keep it true.

Before work ships, check it against the person on the other side of the screen. The brand succeeds when someone finds a trip they love and goes.

Accessibility

Semantic structure, the amber focus ring on everything focusable, AA contrast or better, descriptive alt text, keyboard paths through every rail, and a complete reduced-motion experience.

Claims and proof

No unverified audience numbers, press quotes or testimonials. Do not show stale listing dates as current. Prices are from-prices in USD; say so.

One focus, one way out

Exactly one listing in focus at a time, and at most one amber button in view. If two things are lit, one of them is wrong.

Ownership

DESIGN.md is the source of truth for tokens and rules; PRODUCT.md holds the positioning. Update them, then this guide, as shipped work settles a clearer rule.

  3. Are titles condensed capitals and everything read at width 100?
  4. Does every missing photo have a title card, never an empty frame?
